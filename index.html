The idea

SpaceX was moving toward an IPO, and both the financial press and retail investors were paying attention. Matt McCall had been researching the space sector, so we developed a portfolio of stocks positioned to benefit before and after the listing.

As the IPO approached and coverage intensified, I sharpened the creative around the timing and urgency of the opportunity, shifting the promotion from Elon's private AI holdings to the SpaceX IPO itself.

What I built

I owned both the creative and the infrastructure that distributed it. I scripted, shot and edited the long-form promotional video, built the landing, order and upsell pages, and wrote the promotional emails and the 40+ subject-line angles our partners sent.

I built the affiliate program in Everflow, onboarded and managed the 17 to 20 partners who ran the offer, and built the reporting across beehiiv, Stripe and Everflow that told us where to put volume each week.

How it changed

The campaign drove sales from the first week, but the economics were challenging. Paying partners $200 for each new subscriber on a $49 offer meant front-end revenue alone couldn't support the acquisition cost. Four changes improved the economics.

PriceWe tested $79 against $49. Sales held at $79, so $79 became the offer.
A lifetime upsellSome subscribers had already asked for a lifetime option. Shortly after launch we introduced one at $219, priced below the $298 annual subscription, on the page right after purchase, with a follow-up email a few days later for buyers who passed. 220 subscribers upgraded at 19.7%, materially improving the economics of the campaign.
A back endA lifetime sale on our lowest-tier product could have been the last purchase a subscriber ever made with us. I built further funnels, including The Electrification of Everything campaign into the Early Opportunities tier, so buyers kept moving into other products as the list grew.
Partners and anglesOnce reporting showed which lists converted, we consolidated volume onto the strongest partners. We applied the same approach to creative, putting more volume behind the subject-line angles that won. The tests are below.

What I'd do differently

I'd start with fewer, higher-quality lists. On a CPA deal, every subscriber cost the same $200 regardless of which list they came from, so list quality determined whether that acquisition cost paid back. Starting with a smaller group of proven lists and scaling based on performance would have helped us find the right partner mix sooner, while reducing spend on lower-value subscribers.
